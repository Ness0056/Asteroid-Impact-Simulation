      Detailed validation data for the asteroid lab

===============================================================
Note: the magnitudes with a subindex 0, such as L_0, are meant to
be expressed in the Base or Universal Reference frame (static, at
rest, taken as the t=0 asteroid reference frame with origin at its
center of mass). The magnitudes with a subindex s (self) such as
L_s or w_s are meant to be expressed in the (time varying) reference
frame attached to the asteroid at time t.

The calculations shown here have been computed with a time step
dt = 1.0 . This value is suitable for this check, but the real
calculations once your code is working correctly should be smaller,
such as  dt = 0.25.

The inertia matrix inertia for the asteroid is defined in the code
'before.pov'. You will find there also the mass values of the two 
bodies and the velocity of the incoming meteorite which is going
to hit the asteroid.

The calculation of the total momentum P before the impact is
simply P = Sum [m_i vi ] and it has only a contribution from the
incoming meteorite particle, since the (much bigger) asteroid 
is at rest. After the collision, the total momentum P has not
changed, and since we can write   P = M Vcm (as after the col.
lision we have only one body), we can easily calculate the  
velocity of the center of mass Vcm.

A similar approach allows as to compute L, the total angular 
momentum. Before the impact, we can compute can compute it in
the 0 or Lab reference system at rest:  L = Sum [m_i ri x vi ],
and it has again only a contribution from the incoming meteorite 
particle. After the collision, the total angular momentum L 
has not changed, since in a collision all forces are internal,
and it will conserve its value forever. 

However, in order to relate its value to the angular velocity w,
we will need to work in the "self" reference frame attached to 
the asteroid {S} at its center of mass, which is now at motion
and rotating. Interestingly, all physical vectors as P or L 
transform between different reference frames in the 3-component
form: 

               A     A     B
                L =   R  .  L
                     B 

with R being the 3x3 rotation matrix between the systems A and B:

                     A      
                      R        
                     B 
              {A}   ---->   {B}

This allows us to relate the value of L in the 0 ref. frame to
its value in the "self" CM system, where we have the unique 
expression 

                    L  = (inertia matrix) . w     

with the inertia matrix being constant (see also the notes guide.ps)
